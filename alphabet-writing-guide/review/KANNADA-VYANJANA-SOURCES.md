# Kannada vyanjanas: handwriting evidence and construction

Research and implementation: 31 October 2026. Scope: **34 basic modern consonants, ಕ–ಳ**, including ಙ and ಞ. Together with the existing 15 learning swaras/marks, the app now has 49 Kannada learning characters. ಕ್ಷ and ಜ್ಞ are conjunct constructions and are not silently added to this basic-consonant set.

## What the lesson teaches

Each character uses an ordered list of pencil-centreline paths. One path is one pen-down movement, including a stationary dot where appropriate. The green dot holds for 800 ms before each movement. The pencil travels at a constant distance-based pace, lifts between movements, and leaves exactly the same geometry as the grey guide. There is no font-outline reveal behind the animation. Kannada announcements request `kn-IN`, using the same female-voice preference as the swara set.

The construction follows **one documented handwriting convention**, the Gopala Krishna A animation series also used for the swaras. A published final glyph cannot establish its pen order. These are hand-reconstructed, source-grounded teaching paths; they have not been independently certified by a Kannada handwriting teacher, and this work does not establish a single school-wide rule that makes other legitimate conventions wrong.

[Open the consonant review](kannada-review.html?set=vyanjana). Each letter has its original completed reference beside the reconstruction, numbered pen-down panels, starting points, direction arrows, prompts and playback. Dot taps have no directional arrows. The source-animation link lets a parent or teacher compare the actual movement, not just the final silhouette.

## Reconstruction decisions

- **Identity is checked from the glyph, not the English filename.** The source’s `ta` file depicts ಟ; `tha` depicts ತ; `tta` depicts ಠ; `thha` depicts ಥ; `dda` depicts ಡ; `dhha` depicts ಢ. Some file-page captions are inconsistent (for example the `dhha` caption names ಧ). The implemented mapping follows the actual final form against the primer and JNU inventory. `knha` is ಙ; `chna` is ಞ. These spellings remain only in provenance URLs.
- **Curves are manually reconstructed, not traced around a font.** The editable construction file stores smooth absolute SVG paths in the source GIF’s coordinates. The generator scales uniformly into the app’s 400 × 400 space. Loop proportions, crossing order and separate components are retained; source pixels and moving-pencil coordinates are not represented as exact ground truth.
- **Broad-nib joins need explicit pencil joins.** Thin centrelines exposed gaps hidden by the original thick ink. Body/head attachments were reconstructed in ಕ, ಗ, ಚ, ಛ, ಝ, ಠ, ಡ, ಢ, ತ, ಥ, ದ, ಧ, ನ, ಭ, ಮ, ಯ, ರ, ವ, ಶ, ಸ, ಹ and ಳ. The particular choices are recorded per character in the construction JSON and shown in the review. This includes short extensions into the head stroke; they add no extra lift. Independent dots and intentionally separate parts remain separate.
- **Do not turn a closed loop into a silent retrace.** ಯ closes its left loop, then lifts to the right join for the middle bowl. Its head stroke and final right bowl are separate movements. The lesson uses four movements for this reference.
- **The neck is distinct where the animation makes it distinct.** ಕ and ಚ use four movements here: body, middle bar, short rising neck, upper bar/curl. This preserves the source’s actual breaks; it is not a claim that every school teaches four strokes.
- **Dots are taps.** The inner dots in ಘ/ಙ/ಠ/ಥ/ಪ/ಫ/ಷ/ಸ use a very short centreline with a round cap and a per-stroke width. A nearly stationary mark is not replaced by an invented circular pen journey. Width is identical in the guide, animated ink and review.
- **Lower calligraphic drops become downward pencil marks.** ಘ/ಛ/ಝ/ಡ/ಢ/ಥ/ಫ/ಭ have a distinct downward lower mark. The original thick, tapered silhouette is simplified to a short round-ended pencil stroke in the demonstrated direction. The child sees one movement, not an outline of a teardrop.
- **The Hear control cannot obscure a curl.** Phone review caught an overlap with tall upper curves; the button now sits beside the stroke cue below the canvas.
- **No change of alphabet at completion.** The final letter remains the same paths the child watched. A system font is used only for the small label, never substituted for the large teaching glyph.

These are pencil-style teaching reconstructions rather than facsimiles of broad-nib calligraphy. Their joins and simplified marks are disclosed so that source comparison remains reviewable.

## Validation and reproducibility

- `tools/build-vyanjana.py` generates `kannada-vyanjana.js` from the reviewed construction JSON, using only Python’s standard library. Run it twice to check stable output.
- Optional `--references /path/to/downloaded/gifs` verifies every input SHA-256 and extracts the final-frame crops into `vyanjana-references/`; that optional extraction requires Pillow. The script does not download files. No PDFs or video files are shipped with the activity.
- `tools/check-kannada.cjs` exercises **all 49 Kannada characters at Slow, Normal and Fast**, 800 ms start preparation, constant-distance motion, explicit pen-lift boundaries, solid completed ink, pause/resume/replay, layouts and both review sets. It also verifies all 34 local reference images decode.
- `tools/check-letter-speech.cjs` checks **111 characters across five sets**, Kannada voice requests for all consonants, replay/Hear/navigation/cancellation, and unsupported browsers. Speech is mocked: these checks cannot certify audible pronunciation or the device’s installed voices.
- Rendering, geometry and state-machine checks cannot certify handwriting pedagogy. The reference comparison and explicit reconstruction choices above support parent/teacher inspection.
